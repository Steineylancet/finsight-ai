## Executive Summary  
For FY2026 Q3, the Software Engineering department is forecasted to spend $8.1M, which is $43K (0.5%) below the approved budget of $8.15M. This variance is primarily driven by savings in People Costs and Professional Services, partially offset by higher-than-expected Technology and Facilities & Occupancy expenses. The forecast reflects a disciplined approach to cost management while accommodating strategic investments in tools and infrastructure to support ongoing initiatives.  

---

## Planned Spend by Category  
- **People Costs ($5.24M forecast, $79K under budget, -1.5%)**  
  Savings in People Costs stem from a slower-than-anticipated pace of hiring for planned roles in Q3. While recruitment efforts continue for key positions in cloud architecture and DevOps, onboarding timelines have shifted into Q4.  

- **Technology ($1.65M forecast, $42K over budget, +2.6%)**  
  The forecast includes incremental costs for software licenses and cloud infrastructure to support scaling demands from the new product development pipeline. A mid-quarter renewal of a key software platform has also contributed to the increase.  

- **Professional Services ($650K forecast, $7K under budget, -1.1%)**  
  Slight savings are expected due to renegotiated rates with external consultants supporting the migration of legacy systems.  

- **Travel & Expense ($396K forecast, $4K under budget, -1.0%)**  
  Travel activity remains aligned with budget expectations, though minor savings are anticipated due to a shift toward virtual engagements for certain vendor meetings.  

- **Facilities & Occupancy ($165K forecast, $5K over budget, +2.9%)**  
  Higher costs are attributed to short-term lease adjustments for additional co-working spaces to accommodate hybrid work arrangements.  

---

## Forecast Assumptions & Drivers  
1. **Hiring Delays:** Recruitment for high-demand technical roles is progressing slower than planned, resulting in deferred People Costs.  
2. **Strategic Investments:** Increased Technology spend reflects the prioritization of scalable infrastructure to support product launches and R&D.  
3. **Vendor Negotiations:** Cost efficiencies in Professional Services are driven by favorable contract renegotiations.  
4. **Hybrid Work Model:** Facilities costs are slightly elevated to support flexible workspace needs.  

---

## Risks & Watch Items  
1. **Hiring Market Volatility:** Prolonged delays in filling critical roles could impact project timelines.  
2. **Technology Cost Inflation:** Unanticipated increases in subscription fees or cloud usage could further pressure the Technology budget.  
3. **Project Dependencies:** Delays in key vendor deliverables could necessitate additional Professional Services spend.  
4. **Travel Resumption:** Any uptick in in-person engagements could erode Travel & Expense savings.
