## **Executive Summary**  
For FY2025 Q2, the Executive Department reported total expenses of $2,907,454, exceeding the budget of $2,081,836 by $825,618 (+39.7%). This unfavorable variance was primarily driven by increased People Costs (+$463,795, +37.3%) and Travel & Expense (+$172,170, +45.8%), reflecting strategic investments in leadership initiatives and expanded operational activity. While these variances are notable, they align with ongoing organizational priorities, including talent acquisition, leadership development, and key strategic projects.  

---

## **Expense Performance by Category**  
1. **People Costs**: Actuals were $1,705,561, exceeding the budget of $1,241,766 by $463,795 (+37.3%).  
2. **Travel & Expense**: Actuals were $547,709, exceeding the budget of $375,539 by $172,170 (+45.8%).  
3. **Professional Services**: Actuals were $304,618, exceeding the budget of $210,202 by $94,416 (+44.9%).

---

## **Key Variances & Commentary**  
### **People Costs (+$463,795, +37.3%)**  
The variance in People Costs reflects a combination of factors. First, the department onboarded several senior-level hires in Q2 to support strategic growth initiatives, resulting in higher payroll and benefits expenses. Additionally, vendor costs from Aetna Health Insurance ($438,545) and Cigna Healthcare ($365,307) increased due to rising health insurance premiums and expanded coverage for new hires. Investments in leadership development platforms, such as Cornerstone OnDemand ($329,565), also contributed to the overage.  

### **Travel & Expense (+$172,170, +45.8%)**  
Travel costs surged as executive leadership increased their presence at client meetings, industry conferences, and internal strategy sessions. This reflects a deliberate effort to strengthen client relationships and drive business development. The variance was further amplified by higher-than-anticipated international travel expenses and inflationary pressures on airfare and lodging.  

### **Professional Services (+$94,416, +44.9%)**  
Professional Services costs were elevated due to consulting fees for strategic initiatives, including organizational restructuring and digital transformation projects. Workday Inc. ($319,359) played a key role in these efforts, providing critical support for HR system enhancements.  

### **Facilities & Occupancy (+$51,010, +34.1%)**  
The variance in Facilities & Occupancy was driven by temporary office expansions to accommodate new hires and increased utilization of executive meeting spaces.  

### **Insurance & Risk (+$44,227, +42.1%)**  
Higher-than-budgeted insurance costs stemmed from policy adjustments to address expanded coverage requirements and increased premiums.
