## **Executive Summary**  
The Finance Operations Department closed FY2025 Q2 with total expenses of $2,505,309, exceeding the budget of $1,745,762 by $759,547 (+43.5%). This unfavorable variance was driven by higher-than-anticipated People Costs, Professional Services, and Technology investments. Key drivers include strategic hiring initiatives, increased reliance on external consultants, and technology upgrades to support scalability. While these investments reflect a proactive approach to positioning Crestwood Capital Group for future growth, they require close monitoring in subsequent quarters to ensure alignment with budgetary expectations.

---

## **Expense Performance by Category**  
1. **People Costs**: Actuals of $1,384,214 exceeded the budget of $963,769 by $420,445 (+43.6%).  
2. **Professional Services**: Actuals of $487,369 exceeded the budget of $354,214 by $133,155 (+37.6%).  
3. **Technology**: Actuals of $306,060 exceeded the budget of $204,278 by $101,782 (+49.8%).  
4. **Facilities & Occupancy**: Actuals of $199,066 exceeded the budget of $137,440 by $61,627 (+44.8%).  
5. **Travel & Expense**: Actuals of $128,600 exceeded the budget of $86,061 by $42,539 (+49.4%).  

All categories reported unfavorable variances, with People Costs contributing 55% of the total variance.

---

## **Key Variances & Commentary**  
1. **People Costs (+$420,445 / +43.6%)**:  
   The variance was driven by a strategic headcount expansion to support new business initiatives, particularly in risk management and compliance. Recruitment efforts were accelerated in Q2, reflected in increased spending with vendors like Indeed.com ($620,981) and LinkedIn Talent Solutions ($490,472). Additionally, higher-than-expected benefits costs, including health insurance premiums with Aetna ($418,387) and MetLife ($422,086), contributed to the overrun.  

2. **Professional Services (+$133,155 / +37.6%)**:  
   Increased reliance on external consultants for regulatory compliance projects and technology implementation drove this variance. These engagements were necessary to address immediate operational needs but will taper off in H2 as internal teams take over.

3. **Technology (+$101,782 / +49.8%)**:  
   The variance reflects unbudgeted investments in cloud infrastructure and software upgrades to enhance operational efficiency. These upgrades were accelerated to accommodate the department’s growing data processing needs.

4. **Facilities & Occupancy (+$61,627 / +44.8%)**:  
   The variance was primarily due to temporary office expansions to accommodate the growing workforce. While this is a short-term cost, it underscores the need for long-term space planning.

5. **Travel & Expense (+$42,539 / +49.4%)**:  
   Increased travel activity for client meetings and training sessions post-pandemic recovery drove this variance. This trend is expected to normalize in H2.
