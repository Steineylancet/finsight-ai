## Executive Summary  
The Human Resources (HR) department is projected to close FY2026 Q3 with total expenses of $596,895, exceeding the budget of $583,564 by $13,331 (+2.3%). The primary drivers of this variance include incremental investments in technology, professional services, and facilities to support ongoing HR initiatives. While the forecast reflects a modest overspend across several categories, these investments align with strategic priorities, including scaling workforce capabilities, enhancing employee engagement, and maintaining operational resilience.  

---

## Planned Spend by Category  
- **People Costs:** Forecasted at $297,090, exceeding the budget by $3,063 (+1.0%). This increase reflects planned hiring for two mid-level HR roles to support talent acquisition and employee relations functions.  
- **Professional Services:** Expected to reach $89,102, up $2,233 (+2.6%). The variance is attributed to consulting fees for an external vendor conducting an employee engagement survey and compensation benchmarking analysis.  
- **Technology:** Projected at $89,787, surpassing the budget by $3,724 (+4.3%). This includes the renewal of HRIS software licenses and the rollout of an upgraded learning management system (LMS) to enhance employee training programs.  
- **Travel & Expense:** Estimated at $60,064, exceeding the budget by $1,746 (+3.0%). This reflects increased travel for HR leadership to attend industry conferences and regional office visits to support employee engagement initiatives.  
- **Facilities & Occupancy:** Forecasted at $60,851, up $2,564 (+4.4%). The variance is driven by higher-than-anticipated costs for office space reconfigurations to accommodate hybrid work arrangements.  

---

## Forecast Assumptions & Drivers  
The forecast assumes the successful onboarding of planned hires by mid-Q3 and the timely execution of technology upgrades and professional services engagements. Seasonal factors, such as increased training activity and employee engagement initiatives in the second half of the year, are also incorporated. Additionally, travel and facilities costs reflect Crestwood’s commitment to fostering a collaborative work environment through in-person interactions and optimized office spaces.  

---

## Risks & Watch Items  
1. **Hiring Delays:** Any delays in filling planned roles could impact the department’s ability to execute key initiatives, potentially affecting forecasted People Costs.  
2. **Vendor Dependencies:** The successful delivery of professional services and technology upgrades is contingent on vendor timelines. Any delays or scope changes could result in cost overruns or deferred benefits.  
3. **Facilities Costs:** Continued inflationary pressures in real estate and construction markets may further escalate facilities expenses, warranting close monitoring.  

---

We recommend ongoing collaboration between HR leadership and FP&A to manage these risks and ensure alignment with Crestwood’s strategic objectives.
