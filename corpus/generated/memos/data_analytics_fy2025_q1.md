### **Executive Summary**  
The Data & Analytics department reported total expenses of $643,963 for FY2025 Q1, exceeding the budget of $444,972 by $198,992 (+44.7%). This unfavorable variance was driven by higher-than-anticipated People Costs, Technology investments, and Professional Services expenditures. While these cost overruns reflect strategic initiatives and growth-related activities, they necessitate closer monitoring to ensure alignment with annual financial targets.

---

### **Expense Performance by Category**  
1. **People Costs:** Actuals were $377,651, exceeding the budget of $263,478 by $114,173 (+43.3%).  
2. **Technology:** Actuals were $168,385, exceeding the budget of $114,570 by $53,815 (+47.0%).  
3. **Professional Services:** Actuals were $65,062, exceeding the budget of $45,184 by $19,877 (+44.0%).  
4. **Travel & Expense:** Actuals were $32,865, exceeding the budget of $21,739 by $11,126 (+51.2%).  

All categories reported unfavorable variances, with People Costs and Technology being the primary drivers.

---

### **Key Variances & Commentary**  
1. **People Costs (+$114,173 | +43.3%):**  
   The variance reflects increased headcount in Q1 to support the rollout of the Advanced Analytics Platform (AAP). Hiring activity was expedited to meet project timelines, with significant recruitment costs incurred through LinkedIn Talent Solutions ($303,113) and Indeed.com ($376,180). Additionally, higher-than-expected benefits costs with Aetna Health Insurance ($333,565) and MetLife Insurance ($393,967) contributed to the overage. These costs align with the department's strategic focus on building a robust analytics team but require recalibration of hiring plans for subsequent quarters.

2. **Technology (+$53,815 | +47.0%):**  
   The department invested in cloud infrastructure upgrades and data visualization tools to enhance analytics capabilities. These unbudgeted expenses were necessary to support the AAP and address scalability issues identified during Q4 of FY2024. While these investments are critical for long-term efficiency, future technology spend should be phased to mitigate budgetary impact.

3. **Professional Services (+$19,877 | +44.0%):**  
   The variance stems from increased reliance on external consultants to expedite data migration and integration efforts for the AAP. While this ensured timely project progress, the reliance on third-party expertise exceeded initial assumptions.

4. **Travel & Expense (+$11,126 | +51.2%):**  
   Travel costs were higher than planned due to onsite collaboration requirements for AAP implementation. This included team training sessions and vendor workshops, which were not fully accounted for in the original budget.

---
