### **Executive Summary**  
In FY2025 Q3, the Finance Department reported total expenses of $1,063,283, exceeding the budget of $727,871 by $335,412 (+46.1%). This variance was driven by increased People Costs, Professional Services, and Technology investments, reflecting strategic priorities and unanticipated operational demands. While these investments have positioned us for long-term growth, they underscore the need for tighter cost controls in the coming quarters.  

---

### **Expense Performance by Category**  
1. **People Costs**: Actuals of $599,531 exceeded the budget of $397,830 by $201,701 (+50.7%).  
2. **Professional Services**: Actuals of $205,466 exceeded the budget of $147,013 by $58,453 (+39.8%).  
3. **Technology**: Actuals of $120,720 exceeded the budget of $87,989 by $32,731 (+37.2%).  
4. **Facilities & Occupancy**: Actuals of $84,023 exceeded the budget of $58,649 by $25,374 (+43.3%).  
5. **Travel & Expense**: Actuals of $53,543 exceeded the budget of $36,389 by $17,154 (+47.1%).  

All categories reported unfavorable variances, with People Costs contributing 60% of the total overage.  

---

### **Key Variances & Commentary**  
1. **People Costs (+$201,701 / +50.7%)**  
   The primary driver of this variance was higher-than-anticipated health insurance premiums and payroll expenses. Aetna Health Insurance and ADP Payroll Services accounted for $161,562 and $115,413, respectively, reflecting increased headcount due to mid-year hiring for critical roles in compliance and financial reporting. Additionally, $81,732 was spent with LinkedIn Talent Solutions to support recruitment efforts, as we sought to fill specialized roles in a competitive labor market.

2. **Professional Services (+$58,453 / +39.8%)**  
   The variance reflects consulting fees related to regulatory compliance projects and external audits. These initiatives were necessary to address evolving industry requirements and ensure readiness for upcoming SEC reporting changes.  

3. **Technology (+$32,731 / +37.2%)**  
   Investments in financial systems upgrades and data analytics tools drove the overage. These technology enhancements were critical for improving reporting accuracy and operational efficiency, though implementation costs were higher than initially forecasted.  

4. **Facilities & Occupancy (+$25,374 / +43.3%)**  
   This variance was driven by unplanned office reconfigurations to accommodate new hires and higher utility costs due to increased office utilization.  

5. **Travel & Expense (+$17,154 / +47.1%)**  
   Increased travel was tied to client engagement efforts and attendance at industry conferences, which were prioritized to strengthen relationships and stay ahead of market trends.
