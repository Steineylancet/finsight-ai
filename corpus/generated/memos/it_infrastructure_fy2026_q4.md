## Executive Summary  

For FY2026 Q4, the IT Infrastructure department is forecasted to spend **$4,487,631**, exceeding the budget of **$4,447,342** by **$40,289 (+0.9%)**. This variance is primarily driven by higher-than-expected costs in the Technology category, partially offset by minor savings in People Costs. The forecast reflects anticipated investments in critical infrastructure projects, vendor renewals, and resource optimization initiatives. While the overall variance is modest, careful monitoring of Technology and Professional Services expenses is recommended to ensure alignment with long-term financial objectives.

---

## Planned Spend by Category  

1. **Technology**: Forecasted at **$2,038,679**, exceeding the budget by **$42,925 (+2.2%)**. This reflects higher-than-anticipated costs for cloud infrastructure scaling and software license renewals to support ongoing digital transformation initiatives.  
2. **People Costs**: Forecasted at **$1,549,413**, under budget by **$11,184 (-0.7%)**. This variance is attributed to delayed hiring for two planned roles and lower-than-expected overtime utilization.  
3. **Professional Services**: Forecasted at **$539,589**, slightly over budget by **$3,923 (+0.7%)**. This increase is driven by additional consulting hours required for a data center migration project.  
4. **Facilities & Occupancy**: Forecasted at **$226,086**, exceeding the budget by **$3,264 (+1.5%)** due to higher utility costs and minor unplanned repairs.  
5. **Travel & Expense**: Forecasted at **$133,864**, over budget by **$1,361 (+1.0%)**, reflecting increased travel activity for vendor negotiations and project site visits.  

---

## Forecast Assumptions & Drivers  

- **Technology Investments**: The forecast assumes incremental spending on cloud infrastructure to accommodate increased storage and compute demands. Additionally, software license renewals for critical platforms are expected to occur in Q4.  
- **Staffing**: Hiring for two open positions is now projected to occur in early FY2027, creating temporary savings in People Costs.  
- **Professional Services**: The data center migration project, originally expected to conclude in Q3, has extended into Q4, requiring additional consulting support.  
- **Seasonality**: Year-end vendor negotiations and infrastructure scaling efforts are typical drivers of higher Q4 spending.  

---

## Risks & Watch Items  

1. **Technology Costs**: Unanticipated increases in cloud vendor pricing or additional software needs could further elevate Technology expenses.  
2. **Project Delays**: Any further extension of the data center migration project could increase Professional Services costs beyond the current forecast.  
3. **Facilities Costs**: Unexpected repairs or maintenance could push Facilities & Occupancy expenses higher.
